# Delayed Delivery and Tracking

An order is considered delayed when it has not arrived two business days after the latest estimated delivery date. Before creating a delayed-delivery ticket, confirm that the carrier tracking page has not recorded a delivery attempt, an address problem, or a pickup-point notice.

If tracking has not changed for forty-eight hours and the order is delayed, create a normal-priority delivery ticket. If the order contains a time-sensitive essential item or tracking reports loss or damage, create a high-priority ticket. Do not tell the customer that a parcel is lost unless the carrier status explicitly says so.

When tracking is unavailable or contradictory, escalate to the logistics-support queue with the order ID, last known scan, and customer impact.
